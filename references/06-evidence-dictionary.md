# Evidence Dictionary

## Objective Evidence Types
Common evidence includes documented procedures, policies, records, logs, audit trails, approved plans, risk registers, competence matrices, training records, supplier evaluations, purchase controls, inspection results, release records, NC reports, root cause analysis, corrective action evidence, monitoring data, management review minutes, and internal audit records.

## Evidence Quality Criteria
Evaluate evidence using:
- Relevance to the clause requirement.
- Objectivity and traceability.
- Completeness of required fields.
- Date/freshness and applicable period.
- Approval or authorization status.
- Linkage to process, product, site, or audit scope.
- Consistency across records and interviews.

## Interview Evidence
Interview evidence can support understanding but should not be the sole basis for material conformity unless supported by records or observed practice.

## Evidence Pointer Standard
Use this format when citing evidence:

`<clause>|DOC:<doc_id>|TYPE:<evidence>|PAGE:<p>|LINE:<l1-l2>|SECTION:<sec>|SOURCE:<system>`

If exact page/line is unavailable, use the closest stable locator and state the limitation.

## Evidence Sufficiency
Evidence is insufficient when it is missing, stale, not linked to scope, not objective, inconsistent, incomplete, or only asserted without records/observation.

## v2 Procedure-Only Evidence Limitation
A procedure, policy, or plan shows intended arrangement. It does not by itself prove implementation for clauses that require operation, monitoring, evaluation, control, release, competence, corrective action, or supplier control. When only procedure-level evidence is provided, normally use `InsufficientEvidence` unless the question is limited to document adequacy.

## External Provider Control Evidence Checklist
For ISO 9001 clause 8.4 or supplier/external provider control, request evidence such as:

1. Supplier selection and approval criteria.
2. Approved supplier list or equivalent controlled record.
3. Initial supplier evaluation / approval records.
4. Supplier re-evaluation records at planned intervals.
5. Supplier performance monitoring data, such as delivery, defects, complaints, or responsiveness.
6. Purchase order or purchasing information showing requirements communicated to suppliers.
7. Incoming inspection, verification, certificate review, or acceptance records.
8. Outsourced process control evidence where applicable.
9. Supplier nonconformity, concession, complaint, or corrective action records where relevant.
10. Evidence that changes to supplier status or criteria are controlled and communicated.

## Product Release Evidence Checklist
For ISO 9001 clause 8.6, look for evidence that planned arrangements were completed before release, such as final inspection results, acceptance criteria, release authorization, responsible person/signature, date, batch/order traceability, and any approved concession when requirements were not fully met.

## Corrective Action Evidence Checklist
For ISO 9001 clause 10.2, distinguish:

1. Correction evidence.
2. Root cause analysis evidence.
3. Corrective action plan and implementation evidence.
4. Evidence that actions address the cause.
5. Effectiveness verification and recurrence check.
6. Updated risk/opportunity, procedure, training, monitoring, or control evidence when needed.


## ISO 9001:2026 Evidence Focus

For ISO 9001:2026, check evidence for the following strengthened or new themes:

| Theme | Evidence to request |
|---|---|
| Climate change relevance | context review, issue analysis, interested party requirement review, documented decision if not relevant |
| Quality culture and ethical behaviour | leadership communication, awareness evidence, culture initiatives, interview consistency, behaviour-related records |
| Separated risks and opportunities | distinct risk analysis, opportunity analysis, action plans, effectiveness reviews |
| Strengthened management of change | change purpose, consequence assessment, QMS integrity review, resources/info, responsibility allocation, communication, monitoring/evaluation, result review |
| Organizational knowledge | knowledge needed, retention, application, sharing, updates for changing needs/trends |
| Evidence protection | access control, version control, audit trail, protection from unintended alteration/deletion |
| Product release | acceptance evidence and traceability to release authorizer |

Procedure-only evidence is not enough for implementation-heavy requirements such as 4.4, 6.1.2, 6.3, 7.2, 8.1, 8.4, 8.5, 8.6, 8.7, 9.2, 9.3, and 10.2. Request implementation records, samples, observations, or system evidence.
