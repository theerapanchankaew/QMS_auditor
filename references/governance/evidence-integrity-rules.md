# Evidence Integrity Rules

## Required audit logic
Every material finding or conclusion must include the following logic:

1. Criterion
   - Standard, clause number, internal requirement, or audit criterion
   - Controlled source used

2. Evidence
   - What document, record, interview, observation, sample, or data was reviewed
   - Date, period, process, site, or sample scope when available

3. Condition
   - What was found, what was missing, or what deviated

4. Evaluation
   - Why the evidence does or does not satisfy the criterion
   - Link between requirement element and observed condition

5. Conclusion
   - conformity
   - nonconformity
   - observation
   - opportunity for improvement
   - insufficient evidence
   - ReviewRequired

## Evidence sufficiency rules
- Procedure-only evidence is normally insufficient for implementation-heavy clauses.
- Policy or intent evidence is not enough to prove effective implementation.
- Interview evidence should be supported by records, observation, or sampled outputs where possible.
- Missing evidence alone does not automatically prove nonconformity unless the requirement requires documented information or retained evidence.
- A nonconformity requires a specific requirement breach and objective evidence.

## Prohibited finding behaviour
Do not:

- write a nonconformity without a clear violated requirement
- cite a clause that was not checked
- invent missing records, dates, samples, or statements
- assume implementation from procedure alone
- assume non-implementation from absence of evidence alone
- quote long copyrighted standard text
- use auditor preference as a requirement
- use AHP/risk score alone as proof of conformity or nonconformity

## Confidence labels
Use these labels when giving conclusions:

- `high confidence`: criterion and objective evidence are clear and directly linked
- `medium confidence`: criterion and evidence are generally linked but some context is missing
- `low confidence`: evidence is partial, indirect, or needs human review
- `ReviewRequired`: human auditor or technical reviewer should decide
