# ISO 9001:2026 Requirements Guide for Professional Auditor

## Source and status

Use this guide when the user asks for ISO 9001:2026, ISO/FDIS 9001, transition from ISO 9001:2015, or audit work based on the 2026 revision.

The bundled guidance is based on the user-provided ISO/FDIS 9001 draft. Treat it as draft-stage audit criteria unless the user confirms the final published standard is being used. Do not present this skill as an official copy of ISO 9001:2026. For certification decisions, require verification against the officially issued standard and certification body rules.

## Main 2026 changes to consider

The 2026 draft indicates several key changes from the 2015 edition:

1. Clause 3 includes a limited set of core ISO management system terms and definitions, while ISO 9000 remains the normative quality vocabulary reference.
2. Quality culture and ethical behaviour are introduced into requirements, especially leadership, awareness, and the environment for operation of processes.
3. Risks and opportunities are more clearly separated, with distinct planning for actions to address each.
4. Management of change is strengthened, including implementation effectiveness, communication, monitoring, and review of changes.
5. Annex A is expanded to clarify structure, terminology, and intent without adding requirements.
6. The prior Annex B is removed, with references moved elsewhere.

## Auditor interpretation principles

- Audit against requirements, not against Annex A guidance alone.
- Use Annex A only to clarify intent and terminology; do not create additional requirements from Annex A.
- Treat lists in clauses as applicable as written; do not infer sequence or priority only from item order.
- Distinguish `appropriate`, `applicable`, `consider`, and `take into account`.
- Treat `shall be available as documented information` as information availability, and `documented information shall be available as evidence of` as retained objective evidence.
- Separate risk-based thinking from opportunity-based thinking. Do not merge them into a single vague R&O statement.
- For procedure-only evidence, do not conclude implementation-heavy conformity without records, samples, interviews corroborated by records, observations, or logs.

## ISO 9001:2026 clause focus for audit

### Clause 4 — Context of the organization

Audit focus:
- External and internal issues relevant to purpose, strategic direction, and intended QMS results.
- Explicit determination of whether climate change is a relevant issue.
- Relevant interested parties, their requirements, and which requirements the QMS addresses.
- Scope boundaries, products/services, and justified non-applicability.
- QMS processes, interactions, criteria, methods, resources, responsibilities, risks/opportunities, evaluation, and improvement.

Human-auditor probes:
- How did you determine internal and external issues?
- How did you decide whether climate change is relevant?
- Which interested party requirements are addressed through the QMS?
- Show me the process interaction and evidence that processes operate as planned.

Evidence examples:
- Context review, interested party register, scope statement, process map, KPI definitions, process review records, risk/opportunity outputs, QMS documented information.

### Clause 5 — Leadership

Audit focus:
- Alignment of quality policy/objectives with strategic direction.
- Integration of QMS requirements into business processes.
- Leadership promotion of quality culture, ethical behaviour, process approach, risk-based thinking, and opportunity-based thinking.
- Customer focus and responsibilities/authorities including integrity of the QMS during changes.

Human-auditor probes:
- How does top management demonstrate quality culture and ethical behaviour?
- How are quality objectives linked to strategy?
- How do leaders ensure QMS integrity when changes are planned?

Evidence examples:
- Management interviews, policy/objective deployment, communication records, management review minutes, role descriptions, change governance records.

### Clause 6 — Planning

Audit focus:
- Separate determination, analysis, and evaluation of risks that can have undesired effects.
- Separate determination, analysis, and evaluation of opportunities that can have desired effects.
- Actions integrated into QMS processes and effectiveness evaluation.
- Quality objectives at relevant functions, levels, and processes.
- Strengthened planning of changes including purpose, consequences, QMS integrity, resources, responsibilities, communication, monitoring/evaluation, and review of results.

Human-auditor probes:
- Show how risks and opportunities are determined separately.
- How do planned actions connect to process controls?
- How do you evaluate effectiveness of risk actions and opportunity actions?
- For this QMS change, how were consequences, responsibilities, communication, and effectiveness reviewed?

Evidence examples:
- Risk analysis, opportunity evaluation, action plans, effectiveness review, objective plans, change plans, communication records, post-change review.

### Clause 7 — Support

Audit focus:
- Resources, people, infrastructure, environment, monitoring/measuring resources, organizational knowledge, competence, awareness, communication, and documented information.
- Organizational knowledge must be retained, applied, and shared to the extent necessary.
- Awareness includes relevant quality objectives and organizational quality culture and ethical behaviour.
- Documented information controls include external-origin information and protection of evidence from unintended alteration.

Human-auditor probes:
- What knowledge is necessary for this process and how is it retained, applied, and shared?
- How do personnel demonstrate awareness of quality culture and ethical behaviour?
- How is documented evidence protected from unintended alteration?

Evidence examples:
- Resource plans, maintenance records, calibration/verification records, competence matrix, training/effectiveness records, knowledge capture, awareness materials, communication plan, document control records.

### Clause 8 — Operation

Audit focus:
- Operational planning and control, product/service requirements, design and development, external provider control, production/service provision, release, and nonconforming outputs.
- Clause 8.1 is structurally important: require evidence of implemented controls, acceptance criteria, process criteria, resources, controlled changes, external provision controls, and conformity evidence.
- Clause 8.4 emphasizes accountability for externally provided processes/products/services and risk-based type/extent of control.
- Clause 8.6 is release-sensitive: release cannot proceed until planned arrangements are satisfactorily completed unless approved by relevant authority and customer where applicable; release records need acceptance evidence and release authorization traceability.

Human-auditor probes:
- Show evidence that operational controls were implemented as planned.
- How are acceptance criteria and process criteria defined and applied?
- How are external provider controls determined based on impact and control effectiveness?
- For this released product/service, where is evidence of acceptance and authorization?

Evidence examples:
- Production plans, work orders, inspection records, acceptance criteria, supplier evaluations, purchase orders, incoming inspection, design records, release records, nonconforming output records, concession records, change control records.

### Clause 9 — Performance evaluation

Audit focus:
- Monitoring, measurement, analysis, and evaluation; internal audit; management review.
- Analysis evaluates conformity, customer satisfaction, QMS performance/effectiveness, planning implementation, effectiveness of risk actions, effectiveness of opportunity actions, external provider performance, and need for improvement.
- Internal audit programme considers process importance, previous audit results, and changes affecting the organization.
- Management review includes changes in context/interested party needs and effectiveness of actions for both risks and opportunities.

Human-auditor probes:
- What data do you analyse and how are results used?
- How do you evaluate effectiveness of risk and opportunity actions?
- How does the internal audit programme reflect process importance, previous audit results, and changes?
- What decisions came from management review?

Evidence examples:
- KPI dashboards, analysis reports, customer satisfaction data, internal audit programme/reports, corrective action status, management review inputs/outputs.

### Clause 10 — Improvement

Audit focus:
- Continual improvement considers monitoring/measurement/analysis data and management review results.
- Actions include improving processes/products/services, addressing future needs/expectations, and correcting/preventing/reducing undesired effects.
- Corrective action requires reaction, control/correction, consequences, cause evaluation, similar/potential occurrence check, action implementation, effectiveness review, risk/opportunity updates if needed, and QMS changes if needed.

Human-auditor probes:
- How do analysis and management review results drive improvement?
- For this NC, what was corrected immediately and what action eliminated the cause?
- How did you check if similar NCs exist or could occur elsewhere?
- What evidence shows corrective action effectiveness?

Evidence examples:
- Improvement plans, NC reports, root cause analysis, action plans, effectiveness checks, updated risk/opportunity records, QMS change records.

## ISO 9001:2026 transition audit focus

When asked to support transition from ISO 9001:2015 to ISO 9001:2026, evaluate at least:

1. Clause 4.1: climate change relevance determination.
2. Clause 5.1.1 and 7.3: quality culture and ethical behaviour.
3. Clause 6.1.2 / 6.1.3: separated risk and opportunity processes.
4. Clause 6.3: strengthened management of change evidence.
5. Clause 7.1.6: organizational knowledge retention/application/sharing.
6. Clause 9.1.3 and 9.3: effectiveness of risk and opportunity actions.
7. Clause 10.1: addressing future needs and expectations in improvement.
